Initial counterexamples were reproduced against `39d3a8d4be3e8e2e7de85379790d68f5367d6825`; independent review also found defects in revised implementations. ## Independent review and iteration

Focused regressions first reproduced defects against the baseline. Independent schedules and models then challenged the revised implementations. This exposed additional issues: cancellation plus publication failure on Python 3.14, maximum-wire replay boundaries, ignored PostgreSQL insert allocations, exceptional `raise` prefixes, dataset stride metadata, cross-vehicle cargo, final-verifier stop observation, and persistent changes while rejecting unknown Atlas schemas. Those cases were corrected and rechecked on both supported interpreters.

Bounded no-finding reviews covered Strata's mixed-format torn tails and old/new transfer, Anchor's partial DAG cleanup and real finish/cancel races, Quarry's pin/reader/contract lifecycle, Atlas's Unicode/phrase/ranking/ACL/rebuild behavior, Proving Ground's mathematical/registry/serving/adoption paths, Routecraft's directed schedules and tiny exact plans, and Resonance's partial flush and exact alignment paths. No-finding results describe those probes, not an exhaustive proof of the product.

The repository tooling received the same treatment. Inherited pytest options could conceal a failing test; evidence aggregation could accept the wrong command target or ignore an outer integration failure. Required checks now clear those overrides, read fresh executed JUnit outcomes, retain raw process status, and reject contradictory/incomplete evidence. Integrations explicitly select each participant's project/interpreter/environment, including the nested settlement worker.

## Measurements and limitations

Focused measurements report their exact algorithm/workload scope, source identity, repetitions, correctness checks, timing, and memory/work counters where recorded. Deterministic work reductions are stronger regression checks than elapsed-time assertions. A controlled historical insertion-module comparison is distinct from running an entire historical Routecraft package. The earlier working-tree three-repeat observations remain historical records; the final focused runs use the committed source and explicit repetition counts.

The [final focused record](evidence/core-focused-2026-10-20.json) retains five measured repeats for each main workload comparison, actual imported modules, generated-workload identities, raw observations, and original-artifact hashes. Measurements ran serially under CPython 3.14.8 with numerical threads capped; physical-host exclusivity is not established. Values below are median wall seconds for these workloads.

| Workload | Baseline | Revised | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Current: 12,002 records | 1.4989 | 0.1685 | 72,006,000 candidate comparisons by the baseline full-scan formula versus 6 measured indexed candidates; identical five join pairs and two window results. |
| PyTrace: 900 generated functions | 3.5812 | 0.2485 | Same workload bytes and expected findings; revised analysis also models more control-flow effects. No RSS measurement. |
| Routecraft: 160 customers | 1.2198 | 0.0237 | 1,391,040 full-schedule customer visits versus 13,040; identical independently verified plans. Historical insertion module with revised support code, not a whole-package baseline. |
| Resonance: 600-note alignment | 0.8494 | 0.1288 | Identical mapping hash and defined edit cost; compact traceback payload is not a measurement of total process memory. |
| Resonance: complete 12-second synthetic phrase | 0.3001 | 0.3199 | Revised complete analysis is slower here. Both recover 14 notes/onsets and all 709 eligible frames within ten cents. |

Current's reported peak process RSS increased from 22,282,240 to 23,572,480 bytes. Routecraft additionally ran one paired regret control; that control is not five repetitions. Timing changes are descriptive and do not establish production capacity.

Other focused records preserve their original working-tree identities: [Strata](../projects/02-strata/artifacts/storage-core-review.json) records five repetitions of 30 old-version table probes on a 20,000-version hot key, reducing 1,350 blocks / 87,610,800 bytes per repetition to 6 blocks / 369,136 bytes; [Proving Ground](../projects/08-proving-ground/artifacts/core-lifecycle-review.json) records five bootstrap comparisons avoiding row expansion and repeated score sorting. These are not relabeled as final-commit measurements. Quarry's [independent rebuild exercise](../projects/05-quarry/artifacts/deep-correctness-2026-10-20.json) checks corrections, tombstones, schema changes and retention across seven publications; its single elapsed time is not a throughput claim.

CI-sized workloads and all public integrations are part of the final matrix. Full standard workloads were not rerun. Synthetic workloads do not establish production capacity, search relevance beyond judged queries, predictive accuracy on real equipment, or real-recording transcription quality. PyTrace remains an intraprocedural may-analysis over its documented Python subset; arbitrary heap/interprocedural flow, exception subgroups/types, and context-manager suppression are outside its guarantees. Process termination does not cover every storage/power-loss failure. Model submission remains a trusted local application boundary; statistical sample/asset assumptions and non-cancellable custom loader limits are documented.

Browser checks exercise Atlas and Resonance desktop/mobile behavior, selected accessibility rules, and native playback. They are bounded functional/visual checks, not a complete accessibility assessment. Historical UI/advisory records retain their original source and coverage; unchanged locks do not constitute a complete security certification.
